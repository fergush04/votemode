About VoteMode

How votes are categorized

Every reporting unit's votes are sorted into the method categories its state or county actually publishes. Categories are never inferred: if a source doesn't separate two methods, they stay merged here.

CategoryWhat it covers
Election dayBallots cast in person at a polling place or vote center on election day.
Early in-personBallots cast in person before election day at early-voting sites or vote centers.
In-personIn-person ballots where the source doesn't distinguish election day from early (e.g., Hawaii's voter service centers).
Mail / absenteeBallots returned by mail or drop box and received by the state's deadline.
Late mailMail arriving after election day under a postmark grace period, where reported separately (e.g., Maryland, Virginia).
Pre-election dayAll ballots cast before election day where early in-person and mail can't be separated (e.g., Arizona's "early ballots," Iowa's absentee).
Late pre-election dayPre-election-day ballots processed after election day: Arizona's "late earlies."
ProvisionalBallots held for eligibility review and counted after verification.
AffidavitNew York's name for provisional ballots.
OverseasMilitary and overseas (UOCAVA) ballots reported statewide rather than by locality, shown as a pill at the bottom of the map (Alaska, Maine, Rhode Island).
UnallocatedVotes a source reported without assigning a method. Kept as their own row, never redistributed.
TotalUsed when a unit publishes only a combined total, with no method split at all.

Reporting quality grades

Every county, town, district, and ward carries a grade describing how rich its published method breakdown is. The grade measures the source's reporting, not the accuracy of the votes themselves.

GradeMeaning
C6 – C1 The number of distinct vote methods the source breaks out. A C5 county reports five separate methods (say, election day, early, mail, late mail, provisional); a C1 county reports just one.
UTotals only: the unit publishes a single combined number with no method split. Entire states fall here (e.g., Colorado, Wisconsin, Massachusetts) because their official results carry no breakdown.
FNot yet sourced. Reserved for units with no usable data; none currently on the map.

A state's headline grade is the most common grade among its units. The coverage strip in each state's sidebar shows the full distribution, and the Coverage map view colors every unit by its grade.

Official vs. unofficial results

About 92% of units carry certified official results. The rest were captured from county or state reporting pages ahead of certification and are flagged unofficial in each unit's source note. Every unit records its deviation from the certified total where one exists, and a firm rule applies throughout: numbers are never scaled, corrected, or redistributed to force agreement with certified totals; deviations are recorded and disclosed instead.

Unallocated votes & quality standards

Some counties publish method breakdowns that don't cover every ballot; the remainder is kept as an explicit unallocated row (83 units, mostly in New Mexico, Florida, and New York). Sources are held to these standards before a breakdown is used:

  • A breakdown must reconcile with the certified county total to within about 1%; larger deviations are recorded in the unit's source note, never corrected.
  • A source under-counting the certified total by more than 5% is rejected and flagged for re-sourcing.
  • A county whose breakdown leaves the majority of votes unallocated is treated as totals-only (U) rather than presented as a meaningful split.
  • Merged reporting units are shown as their sources report them, e.g., Maine's Albion/Unity Twp, rather than split by estimate.

Known gaps & quirks

  • Kalawao County, HI (18 votes) is blank: its result is reported merged into Maui in the source data and no official method split exists.
  • Vermont's unincorporated gores and grants (Buels Gore, Averill, and seven others) are grey; they have no voters.
  • Statewide ballots that belong to no geography (Alaska's federal overseas district, Maine's UOCAVA ballots, Rhode Island's federal-only ballots) appear as a clickable pill below the map.
  • California offers early voting in most counties, but counties report it inside their mail and election day columns, so it can't be separated (see California's state profile).
  • Rhode Island's "limited" ballots (presidential-only ballots from late registrants) are folded into their municipalities, matching how every other town reports them.
